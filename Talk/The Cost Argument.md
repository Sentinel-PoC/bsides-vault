# The Cost Argument

Core thesis of the talk. Section 4 of [[Talk Arc]].

## The Old Way

To build what Jim has without AI, five years ago:
- Platform/infrastructure engineer (OpenShift/OKD): $130-150k
- Security engineer (Wazuh/CrowdSec/Kyverno/Vault): $120-140k
- CI/CD and GitOps specialist: overlap or third person
- GRC analyst (SSP, SAR, POA&M, 366 controls): $80-100/hr contract
- Timeline: 6-9 months with experienced people
- **Conservative total: $400-500k annual labor + 6 months**

## The New Way

- $200/month AI subscription
- [[Hardware]] already owned
- Jan 20, 2026 to present — original sprint ~11 weeks; platform in active daily use through talk day

## The Framing (Honest, Not Hype)

"I'm not claiming my platform is as good as what a fully staffed enterprise team would build. But it's as good as or better than what most organizations *actually have* — and most of them spent orders of magnitude more to get there."

## The Steak Analogy

"This isn't filet mignon at filet prices. It's filet at New York strip prices."

## Evidence

- [[Repo Map|12 repos]], [[Commit Stats|~2,924 commits]] (as of June 2, 2026)
- [[Deployed Apps|31 apps]] running
- [[NIST 800-53 Compliance|366 NIST controls]] assessed
- [[Security Stack|10+ security tools]]
- [[SSP]] + [[SAR]] + [[POA&M]] = 2,379 lines

## The Close Version

"When doing it right costs $200 instead of $4 million, the excuse collapses. You can't sit in a boardroom and say you accept the risk because the remediation costs two hundred dollars."
